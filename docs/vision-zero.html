About this dashboard

A public map of traffic crashes on Houston city streets that killed or seriously injured someone, for anyone working on traffic safety in Houston. Data & methods

Data

Crash records from the Texas Department of Transportation (CRIS). Street network from OpenStreetMap; street design, boundaries and the High Injury Network from City of Houston GIS.

Feedback and source

Data & methods

What's shown

Traffic deaths and serious injuries on surface streets in the City of Houston, 2016 to 2025 and part of 2026, from TxDOT's public crash records (CRIS). The map covers the City's full-purpose area. Limited-purpose annexation areas are not included.

Which roads

By default the dashboard leaves out freeways, tollways, ramps and frontage roads. It keeps major streets whoever owns them, including state roads such as S Main (US-90A), SH 6 and Westheimer (FM 1093). Austin's Vision Zero dashboard and the City's High Injury Network do the same.

Set Freeways to Included to add crashes on freeways, toll roads, ramps and frontage roads. They are added to every total and chart and appear on the map as dots. Toll roads are run by the county toll authority, so they count only under Road owner: All. Crashes in parking lots and on private property are never included.

Road ownership

Each street and crash is labeled city-owned or TxDOT-owned, using TxDOT's roadway inventory. A street counts as TxDOT-owned only where it runs along a state road. A city street that crosses over a freeway stays city-owned.

How crashes map to streets

Each crash is assigned to its nearest street within 200 ft (median 4 ft).

Definitions

Killed or seriously injured (KSI) = the K + A levels on the KABCO injury scale.

Years of life lost = years before age 75, added up for everyone killed (the CDC method), using each person's age from the crash report.

Age and sex = from the crash report for each person killed or seriously injured. As a filter, they keep crashes where at least one person in that group was killed or seriously injured.

Lighting = the light condition the officer recorded. Dark includes lighted and unlighted streets.

Contributing factors = factors the officer recorded for any driver or vehicle in the crash. Speeding includes unsafe speed, speeding over the limit and failing to control speed. Failed to yield covers all right-of-way violations by a driver. Alcohol or drugs includes had been drinking and under the influence. Distracted includes inattention and phone use.

Rate by neighborhood income = people killed or seriously injured in crashes in the block groups at each income level, divided by the people who live in those block groups, per 100,000 residents per year. Population is from the 2023 American Community Survey. Block groups that cross the city line count only the share of residents inside it, by land area.

Neighborhood income = median household income of the Census block group where the crash happened.

Counts, not rates

Streets are ranked and shaded by the number of people killed or seriously injured. The City's High Injury Network is built the same way. The counts are not adjusted for traffic volume, because most local streets have no traffic counts. On busier streets that do have counts, the results are about the same after adjusting for traffic.

Crash costs

Each person hurt in a crash is given the FHWA per-person cost for their injury level (FHWA-SA-25-021, 2024 dollars). Economic cost covers medical care, lost work, property damage, and emergency, legal and insurance costs. Victims, insurers, employers and government all pay part of it.

The comprehensive cost also puts a dollar value on lost quality and length of life. It overlaps with years of life lost, so the two should not be added together.

A crash that hurts several people counts each person. Injuries with no recorded severity have no cost, and some crashes are never reported, so these figures are a minimum. Costs are national averages.

Data sources

All data is public.

Crashes: TxDOT Crash Records Information System (CRIS), public extracts, 2016–2025 plus partial 2026. txdot.gov, cris.txdot.gov

Street network & sidewalks: OpenStreetMap. openstreetmap.org

Road ownership (city vs TxDOT): TxDOT Roadway Inventory. TxDOT Open Data Portal

Street design (speed, lanes, width, median) & traffic volume: City of Houston Public Works. COH GIS Open Data

High Injury Network overlay: City of Houston Vision Zero, official 2022 HIN. houstontx.gov/visionzero

Neighborhood income: U.S. Census American Community Survey, 2023 5-year estimates (block group). census.gov/acs

Crash costs: FHWA, Updated Crash Costs for Highway Safety Analysis (FHWA-SA-25-021), KABCO per-person costs in 2024 dollars. highways.dot.gov

City boundary, council districts, super neighborhoods, TIRZs, management districts, HPD patrol divisions, ZIP codes: City of Houston GIS. COH GIS Open Data

Schools: Texas Education Agency school directory (public district and charter campuses), via City of Houston GIS. Parks: City of Houston Parks and Recreation, via City of Houston GIS.

Census tracts: U.S. Census Bureau TIGERweb (2020 tracts). tigerweb.geo.census.gov

Layer names, dates and methods are listed in the project CODEBOOK on GitHub.
